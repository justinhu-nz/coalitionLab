Method

How this works

  • Party vote. A weighted average of polls from the last four weeks. Newer polls count more (weight halves every 7 days), bigger samples count more, and 1 News–Verian and Taxpayers' Union–Curia get a slight (10%) bump. Before averaging, each poll is corrected for its firm's house effect: how far that firm has leaned from the all-firm consensus over 2020–2026 (The Overhang). For example, Verian has run 1.5 points high on National and Reid Research 1.7 high on Labour. This corrects for which firms happen to have polled lately; it can't correct for all the pollsters missing in the same direction, which the polling error below covers.
  • Polling error. Each simulation adds a shared swing between the blocs (typically ±1.5 points), error for each party scaled to its size (about ±2.2 points for Labour and National), and extra error for Te Pāti Māori and TOP, whose polling is least reliable. That roughly matches past misses: 1 News–Verian's final poll has averaged 1.2 points of error per party since 2002, but Labour and National were each out by 4–5 points in 2020.
  • Special votes. Each simulation is counted twice. The final count includes special votes; the election-night count removes them. Their lean is the average of 2020 and 2023 (in 2023 the Greens rose 7.6% from night to final, TPM 18%, TOP 7%, while National fell 2.4% and NZ First 5.9%), scaled down by 30% because election-day enrolment, a big source of left-leaning specials, ended in 2026. Each simulation varies the lean by ±50%.
  • General electorates. Only National's count matters for the outcome, because it decides whether National gets overhang seats. Each seat's 2023 notional party vote is swung toward the poll, converted into candidate votes using how each party's voters split their ticket in 2023 (62% of ACT voters and 30% of NZ First voters gave National their electorate vote), and each candidate keeps their 2023 personal vote. The same method as the electorates page.
  • Māori electorates. Starting shares from the Whakaata Māori–Curia polls (see the table), noise of ±8 points per candidate, and a shared Te Pāti Māori swing averaging +4, so a good night for TPM tends to be good everywhere.
  • Seats. The same Sainte-Laguë allocation, 5% threshold, electorate lifeline and overhang rules as the seat calculator. The code is shared.
  • Outcomes. Left is Labour + Greens + Te Pāti Māori + Te Tai Tokerau Party. Right is National + ACT + NZ First. If neither has a majority but TOP or the independent can give one, that's kingmaker. Otherwise it's deadlock: only a cross-bloc deal or a new election.

Sources: party polls · Whakaata Māori–Curia electorate polls · Newsroom on the Māori seats · 2023 special votes · 2020 special-vote ratios · 1 News poll accuracy · Electoral Amendment Act 2025 · 1 News on National's overhang.

Not modelled: new candidates or retirements in general seats, Kerrin Leoni standing as an independent while on the ballot as Labour (treated as Labour), a lifeline electorate for TOP or NZ First, ACT or Greens overhang, or changes in electorate results between election night and the final count. The error sizes are set by hand to roughly match recent New Zealand misses, not fitted.
